## What a Company Is

A Company is a user-managed, named group of robots within one locale. Its only job is to let every editable Robot Options field be edited across all of its member robots at once instead of one robot at a time. A robot belongs to at most one Company; a robot with no `companyId` is **Freelance** — the implicit default, not a distinct flag or a member of some hidden "no company" company.

```ts
// src/types/Company.ts
interface Company {
  id: string;
  name: string;
  robotIds: string[];
  lastEditedOptions?: CompanyOptionsSnapshot;
}
```

`CompanyOptionsSnapshot` mirrors every editable field the four Robot Options sections expose — Audio Setting, Volume + its LFO, and the full contents of the Ping Controls, Ping Contour, and Signature Array drawers. It deliberately excludes the read-only Display rows (Name/Job/Battery/Docking) and the Reset Melody action, neither of which has a company-scoped meaning. Every field on it is optional — see "The Snapshot Merge" below for why. This includes `clickTrackActive` — Ping Controls' Click Track testing toggle (see [MELODY_SYSTEM.md](MELODY_SYSTEM.md)'s Click Track note) — which, unlike Reset Melody, *does* have a company-scoped meaning (broadcasting it puts every member's playback into click-track mode at once) even though its own control only ever renders behind `DEV_TUNING`.

## Spawn-Time Generation

Companies are seeded once per locale, the same deterministic way the rest of a fresh locale is — no `crypto.randomUUID()`, no `Math.random()` outside the noise-map/seed utilities [PROCEDURAL_GENERATION.md](PROCEDURAL_GENERATION.md) documents. The membership is drawn **before any robot spawns** (Company Families, backlog 73, [docs/specs/NO_SILENCE_AND_COMPANY_FAMILIES.md](specs/NO_SILENCE_AND_COMPANY_FAMILIES.md) §1.11), so a company can spawn as a family (below). `generateCompanyMembership(noiseMap, rosterSize = MAX_ROBOTS)` (`src/systems/spawnSystem.ts`, pure) draws 2-3 companies (`INITIAL_COMPANIES_MIN`/`_MAX`), each a seeded 3-4 robots (`COMPANY_SIZE_MIN`/`_MAX`), as lists of roster indices taken from a shrinking pool so no robot is ever claimed twice (even draws, `getUniformSeededVal`, at `company.count`, `company.size` and `company.member`, so the count splits 2 / 3 and each size 3 / 4 evenly and no roster index is favoured; [docs/specs/EVEN_WORLD_DRAWS.md](specs/EVEN_WORLD_DRAWS.md)). **At least two robots stay Freelance** (`FREELANCERS_MIN = 2`, `src/constants/index.ts`): a company takes at most the pool less two, and a company that cannot then reach three is not made, nor is any after it. So a drawn third company is made only when the first two do not both take four, and about 38 % of worlds have three companies, 62 % two. Over the 121 grid worlds that floor touches 27: in 16 the third company is not made, in 11 it is cut from four robots to three; every world still has two companies, and 46 have three (measured 2026-11-11 for Even World Draws; on the plain draws it was 34, 18, 16 and 53, [docs/tasks/COMPANY_FAMILIES.md](tasks/COMPANY_FAMILIES.md), Results). `spawnInitialRoster` reads the membership to spawn each company's members; `spawnInitialCompanies` — a separate function, called immediately after it from `worldTransition.ts`'s `initializeLocale`, inside the same `locale.robots.length === 0` guard — makes the companies from `generateCompanyRosterBaseline` (the same indices turned into this roster's ids, the replay the session diff uses, so the live companies and the replayed ones agree by construction), each given a generated name via `generateCompanyName` (Adjective + Noun, the same mechanism `generateRobotName` uses, paired with a distinct `COMPANY_NOUNS` list so a company can never generate the exact word-pair a robot name can). A locale loads with a meaningful chunk of its roster already grouped — and a meaningful chunk left Freelance — before a user touches anything. `MAX_COMPANIES` (`= 6`, `src/constants/index.ts`) is a separate ceiling on top of this, capping how many companies can exist at once (spawn-generated plus anything created by hand afterward via `CompanyCrudControls`) — it's never read by spawn generation itself.

Because `spawnInitialCompanies` hooks into the exact same guard `spawnInitialRoster` does, Companies inherit `worldTransition.ts`'s existing retransmit semantics for free, with no company-specific branching: a coordinates-changing retransmit builds a genuinely new, empty locale (robots *and* companies regenerate fresh), while an Attenuation-Style-only retransmit re-parents the *existing* locale untouched (robots *and* companies both survive unchanged). Nothing about a company — membership, name, its settings snapshot — is meant to survive a coordinates reseed; this is deliberate, matching how nothing else in the locale survives one either, and reading as part of the same "new world" event lore-wise. A theme change on the off screen (`retransmitWorld`'s `loadingOnly` mode, roadmap 57) rebuilds nothing either: the companies stand, and the deal below is drawn again over them in place, under the old loading and the new, with only the fields that moved written to each robot (`rederiveLoading`, [docs/tasks/OFF_SCREEN_FINAL_FIXES.md](tasks/OFF_SCREEN_FINAL_FIXES.md) F7). A change on any of the four aspect family rows rebuilds nothing: the companies stand and the deal moves over the live roster (`aspectsOnly`, below).

### A seeded company is a family (Company Families, backlog 73)

Every seeded company is one **founder** and its **members**, so it sounds as a section: one voice, one rhythm, several
parts. The founder is the company's lowest roster index, the robot that spawns first (`companyFounders(membership)`,
`spawnSystem.ts`: member index → founder index, the one reading of the membership for spawn and the baselines). A
Freelance robot is always an original: the 30 % roll that once let any robot copy an earlier one (`robot.copyChance`,
`robot.copySource`) is retired, and nothing draws either key.

- **A member inherits** (`spawnRobot(localeId, { …, founderIndex })`, which reads the founder from the store at that
  index): `audioAttributes` (the voice), `octaveRange`, `rhythmicDensity`, `rhythmicMotifLength`, `noteVariance`,
  `pitchRepeat`, `dynamics`, `rests`, `accents`, `lfoLinks` and the five sends (`reverbSend`, `delaySend`,
  `gritSend`, `shimmerSend`, `doubleSend`), so a section sits in one space.
- **A member keeps its own** id, name, identity colour, gem, `compositionSeed`, master volume (drawn as an original's,
  over the inherited register), pan (Auto at centre, as every robot starts), battery size and start state. Its own
  composition seed gives it its own notes; its own battery and start mean a company lines up now and then and parts
  again, as the lifecycle moves it ([ROBOT_LIFECYCLE.md](ROBOT_LIFECYCLE.md)).
- **The rhythm follows from the settings.** A rhythm belongs to the world and the rhythm shape, the onsets that a
  robot's Density and Motif Length make of a measure ([MELODY_SYSTEM.md](MELODY_SYSTEM.md), "The rhythm stream"), so
  a member, whose density and motif are its founder's, plays the founder's onsets and note lengths exactly; with the
  same Rests and Accents (inherited too) it rests and accents with it. Nothing about the family is in the melody
  recipe.
- **The lineage.** A member carries `lineage: { founderIndex }` (`Robot.lineage`, `src/types/Robot.ts`); founders and
  freelancers carry none. Only the deal reads it (below). It is set at spawn, never edited, never shown and never
  diffed into a session (regenerated from the seed), and a robot moved by hand into another company keeps it.
- **The baselines mirror spawn.** `generateRobotRosterBaseline` gives a member its founder's baseline with its own
  name and volume (`generateRobotAudioBaseline` is an original's only), so an untouched world still saves as
  `robotOverrides: {}` ([SESSION_STORAGE.md](SESSION_STORAGE.md)).

Over the 121 grid worlds, 4 to 7 robots a world are members (mean 5.84; 5.95 on the plain draws), so a world has between 5 and 8 distinct
voices. The Robot Load cap of the Audio Load Budget stays first come, first served, so at a low cap a company may
sound as part of a section.

**Joining a rhythm by hand.** Membership and rhythm are separate things. Moving a robot into a company changes nothing
it plays (broadcast-not-link, below). Setting a robot's Density and Motif Length so they make a company's shape makes
it play that company's rhythm, family or not, and changing them takes it out again; a company-wide Density or Motif
Length broadcast puts every member on one shape, so on one rhythm. The same holds by chance: in one world any two
robots that draw the same shape play together, and before families 66 % of robots already shared a rhythm with
another (Task 3a's Run, [docs/tasks/COMPANY_FAMILIES.md](tasks/COMPANY_FAMILIES.md), Results; accepted by Crawford).

### The deal (roadmap 57, docs/specs/OFF_SCREEN.md assumptions 21, 22 and 24)

Each of the off screen's six aspect family rows — Melody, Envelope, Distance, Voice, Motion and Performance (Motion and Performance since roadmap 58, `docs/specs/THEME_AND_ASPECTS_REFINEMENT.md`) — picks a **set** of aspects for the world, in canonical order, and the fleet's seeded groups are how one world carries several. `dealAspects(identity, companies, roster, noiseMap)` (`src/data/bands/dealAspects.ts`, pure) takes the groups in **deal order** — each company's members, the companies in spawn order, then every Freelance robot on its own in roster order (`dealGroups`; membership is read from the companies' `robotIds`, not from `robot.companyId`, so the regenerated baseline, whose robots carry no `companyId`, groups as the live roster does) — and for each family with a non-empty pick list rotates the canonical list by a seeded offset (one `getUniformSeededVal` at `aspects.<family>.deal`, uniform because a plain draw at offset 0 takes only a few values across worlds) and deals group *i* pick `(i + rotation) mod n`. One pick is the whole fleet; two alternate across the companies then the freelancers. There is no `'theme'` pick (removed 2026-10-31, `docs/tasks/OFF_SCREEN_REVISIONS.md` Task 6, Crawford's note 4): a family with picks deals one to every group, so a fleet no longer mixes plain robots with an aspect's, and a family with none leaves every robot as the theme in it. Each family turns on its own rotation, so one robot can carry an aspect from every family. Deterministic: a link reproduces the deal; adding or removing a pick may reshuffle which company has which, and is a retransmit anyway.

The deal is applied by **re-deriving** each dealt robot's family fields from its **deal index** under the aspect's bands — a company member's founder's spawn index (`lineage.founderIndex`), anyone else's own (Company Families, Task 6) — through the derive function spawn itself uses — `deriveMelodyFields` ([MELODY_SYSTEM.md](MELODY_SYSTEM.md)), `deriveEnvelopeFields`, `deriveSendFields`, `deriveVoiceFields`, `deriveMotionFields` or `derivePerformanceFields` ([AUDIO_SYSTEM.md](AUDIO_SYSTEM.md), the band table), over `resolveBands(theme, { [family]: aspect })` — and a robot dealt several families takes them in family order. `Robot.loadedUnder` (`FamilyAspects`, `src/data/bands/aspects.ts`: per family, one of that family's own ids; JSON) records the aspects. It runs in two places from the same noise map so they agree field for field: **live**, `applyAspectDeal(localeId)` in `initializeLocale` right after `spawnInitialCompanies`, writing each dealt robot through `updateRobot`, then handing each dealt family to the engine through the call a hand edit of its fields makes (Melody `regenerateMelody`, Envelope `AudioEngine.updateVoiceEnvelope`, Distance `AudioEngine.updateRobotSends`, Voice `AudioEngine.reReserveVoice` then `primeRobotLinks`, Motion `AudioEngine.updateVoicePortamento` then `primeRobotLinks`, Performance `regenerateMelody`; revisions Tasks 8 and 10, refinement T7b); and **in the baseline**, `applyDealToBaselines` inside `generateDealtRosterBaseline`, which `buildSessionPayload` diffs against — so an untweaked dealt world saves and shares as `robotOverrides: {}` ([SESSION_STORAGE.md](SESSION_STORAGE.md)). So a family stays one voice under any pick: the founder (the lower index) is dealt first, a member's Motion lanes are tallied as its founder's (`priorLanesOf` at the deal index, so its links are the founder's), and `applyDealToBaselines`, whose baselines carry no lineage, reads the same founders from the seeded membership (`companyFounders(generateCompanyMembership(noiseMap, robotIds.length))`). A robot moved into a company later keeps what it rolled — the deal is never re-run on a Move. A family row's or a theme's change re-derives the live roster in place (`rederiveLoading`, `aspectsOnly` / `loadingOnly`; [OFF_SCREEN_REVIEW_FOLLOWUPS](tasks/OFF_SCREEN_REVIEW_FOLLOWUPS.md) R33, field by field since [OFF_SCREEN_FINAL_FIXES](tasks/OFF_SCREEN_FINAL_FIXES.md) F16): the new picks are dealt over the locale's **live** companies and robots as `applyAspectDeal` deals them, the old side is what each robot was dealt (its `loadedUnder`), and each robot takes only the fields whose seeded value the two disagree on — an aspect's own bands' fields, a family no longer dealt going back to the theme's own draw — so a tweak on any other field stands. A robot's family changes only when the change moves the aspect its company is dealt; every other family keeps its own `loadedUnder`. So a robot moved to another company keeps what it rolled until a change moves its new company's pick, and then takes that pick, derived at its own deal index: a moved member keeps its lineage, so its founder's index, not its new company's founder's (B15) ([OFF_SCREEN_FINAL_FIXES](tasks/OFF_SCREEN_FINAL_FIXES.md) F20; Crawford, 2026-11-01: "nothing about the robot should change unless the robot itself or its new company gets updated"). The session baseline deals over the seeded companies, so such a robot's fields can differ from the baseline, and `buildSessionPayload` saves them as overrides, which round-trip. Each moved family reaches the engine through the same calls, with no rebuild and no wash.

**Readable in the app** (assumption 24). The company screen's detail column and the robot screen's avatar column each carry one `DualLabel` line, "Loaded Under" (`company.loadedUnder` / `probe.loadedUnder`, stand-in copy), over the aspect's name: the robot's reads its own `loadedUnder`; the company's shows the entry every member shares (`describeLoadedUnder` in `src/utils/loadingNames.ts` joins a record's families with ` + `), and shows nothing when members disagree, when none carries one, or on the All Probes form. Text, not a control: the aspect is where a fresh robot started, not a live setting.

## Selection & Highlighting

`uiStore.ts` tracks `selectedCompanyId: string | null` and `allRobotsSelected: boolean`, independent of `selectedRobotId` — selecting a robot never touches either. The two are mutually exclusive: `selectCompany` always clears `allRobotsSelected`, and `selectAllRobots` always clears `selectedCompanyId`. `allRobotsSelected: true` (the default) is the "no company selected" state, not a bulk-edit mode; the bulk edit over the whole fleet is a separate flag, `allProbesSelected` (the **All Probes** form, below). Picking goes through `navActions.selectEntity` ([UI_SHELL.md](UI_SHELL.md)), which starts from a clean slate and then sets exactly one of: a robot, a company, or All Probes. Selecting a company:

- Opens the **company screen** (`CompanyScreen`) on that company.
- Glows each member robot in the world view — `Robot.tsx`'s `isCompanyMember` (`robot.companyId === selectedCompanyId && selectedCompanyId !== null`) reuses the exact same `.robot.selected` CSS declaration single-robot selection already defines (`OceanScene.css`), and sets the robot's `--halo` to the company's colour (a selected robot's halo is its own identity colour, and wins when both apply). `isCompanyMember` and `isSelected` are independent and can both apply to the same robot at once.
- Shows its line in the Deck's meta box (name, member count) — [UI_SHELL.md](UI_SHELL.md).

**All Probes** activates the same form over every robot in the locale regardless of company (Freelance included) — added as a testing convenience (checking a broadcast edit across the whole locale used to mean selecting each company one at a time; see [BPM_CONTROL.md](tasks/BPM_CONTROL.md)'s "Post-launch addition: Company 'All' broadcast mode") and kept as a general bulk-edit option. It is `CompanyScreen` with `target = ALL_TARGET` (`'all'`), which `CompanyScreen` takes whenever no company is selected. Unlike a specific company, All Probes does **not** glow every robot in the world view — `isCompanyMember` is false for everyone, since a highlight on every robot would mark nothing apart. It has no `Company` object to bind to, so Rename / Delete exist only on a specific company's screen. Deleting the selected company lands on the roster board (`CompanyRenameDeleteForm` calls `selectBranch('probes')`); a world change that regenerates companies with fresh ids clears the company (`retransmitWorld` calls `selectAllRobots()`, the no-company state), so Probes shows the roster board; a selected company dropping to zero members instead leaves it selected, with the panel disabled (below).

## Where Companies Live in the UI

Three surfaces, all under the Probes branch of the shell described in [UI_SHELL.md](UI_SHELL.md); none of the pre-rework surfaces (the robot card list, the company button row, the CRUD sidebar) exist any more, and neither does the Companies box in the nav panel, which left in backlog 71 ([plan](tasks/OFF_SCREEN_CONSOLE.md) Phase 5b, Crawford, 2026-11-09).

1. **The roster board** (`RosterBoard`, the Probes page) — the head holds All Probes and `+ New Company`, which discloses `CompanyCreateForm` (`src/components/company/CompanyCrudControls.tsx`): a locally-staged name draft pre-filled by a fresh `generateCompanyName` suggestion, fed by `Math.random()` rather than a seeded noise map since it is a live UI convenience roll, not replayable world generation; disabled at `MAX_COMPANIES`. Then one row per group from `rosterRows({ robots, companies })` (`src/utils/rosterRows.ts`): the companies in order, then **Freelance** last, always present even when empty; a robot's group is its `companyId` in locale robot order, and an orphan or empty `companyId` reads as Freelance. Each `RosterTile` shows the gem, the name, a status line and a battery strip, and beside it a **Move chip** (see "Company Membership").
2. **The company screen** (`CompanyScreen`) — opened from a company's name box on the roster board, under Probes (`selectEntity({ companyId })`: the Probes box popped, the breadcrumb Root / Probes / <Company> / <section>, the Probes box or the breadcrumb's Probes back to the board) — the robot screen with its avatar column swapped for a company column: the name as a popped box in the company's colour, the share and signal wells, the members as gem tiles that open that robot, a broadcast note, and Rename / Delete (`CompanyRenameDeleteForm`: Rename is a staged-draft form, disabled for a blank draft or one unchanged from the current name; Delete sits behind a Radix `AlertDialog` confirmation that portals into `ScreenViewport`). The controls are the robot screen's `SectionTabs` over the same four sections.
3. **The robot screen** (`RobotScreen`) — its avatar column carries the company radio, the second place a robot's company is changed.

### The bulk-edit controls: `useCompanyOptions`

`useCompanyOptions(target)` (`src/components/company/useCompanyOptions.ts`) returns `{ company, members, options }`: `target` is a company id or `ALL_TARGET`; `options` is one memoized bag of the resolved values and the broadcasting change handlers; `company` and `members` sit outside it so the bag's identity changes only when a value does. `CompanyOptionsSection.tsx` is now only `useCompanySubsectionRenderer(options)` — the function that renders one subsection from the bag — and `companyTraitStyle`; `CompanyScreen` calls the renderer inside its tabs. The four sections are the exact same presentational ones the robot screen renders (Audio Setting + Volume, Ping Controls, Ping Contour, Signature Array), each with a `value`/`onChange`/`disabled` contract, no `robot` prop and no store access of its own; the read-only Name/Job/Battery/Docking rows have no company-scoped meaning and are not part of it.

With a selected company that has zero members, every section renders `disabled` with a placeholder value (structurally complete — e.g. 3 signature-array layer slots, not an empty array — so the layout doesn't jump). With a non-empty company selected, or All Probes over a non-empty locale (always the case in practice — the roster is fixed at 12), each section's value comes from `resolveCompanyOptions` (see below).

## The Snapshot Merge

`resolveCompanyOptions(lastEditedOptions, firstMember)` (`src/systems/companyOptions.ts`) is what makes "revert to the last state it was in when last editing, or the first robot's options if unused" true without a special-cased first-edit branch. `lastEditedOptions` is `Company.lastEditedOptions` for a selected company, or `Locale.allRobotsLastEditedOptions` for "All" — the function itself doesn't know or care which:

```ts
return { ...fromFirstMember, ...lastEditedOptions };
```

Every field the company has never been edited for falls back live to `firstMember`'s (the company's first member robot's) *current* value — not a value frozen at whenever the company was first selected. A field the company *has* been edited for reads from its own recorded `lastEditedOptions` instead, regardless of what the first member has since drifted to individually. Re-selecting a company after switching away and back always shows exactly what was last dialed in for it, field by field — this is a deliberate simplification over literally cloning every field into `lastEditedOptions` at first-edit time (functionally identical from the user's perspective; see `docs/specs/COMPANIES.md` §7.1 for the reasoning), and it's why `CompanyOptionsSnapshot`'s fields are all optional rather than a fully-populated snapshot.

## Editing Semantics: Broadcast, Not Link

A company-scoped edit is a one-time broadcast, not a live binding. Changing a field on the company screen calls the matching function from `src/systems/robotOptionsActions.ts` — `applyAudioMode`, `applyVolume`, `applyPan`, `applyPanAuto`, `applySend` (any of the five sends, by field), `applyPortamento`, `applyBusFilter`, `applyDensity`, `applyPitchRepeat`, `applyMotifLength`, `applyNoteVariance`, `applyOctaveMin`, `applyOctaveMax`, `applyClickTrackActive`, `applyDynamics`, `applyRests`, `applyAccents`, `applyAdsr`, `applyLayersContinuous`, `applyLayersStructural`, `applyLayerLfoLink` (there is no Volume LFO action any more) — once per current member robot. These are the exact same functions the robot screen calls once for a single robot; nothing about what an edit *does* differs between the two call sites, only how many robots it's called for. Skipping the matching `AudioEngine`/`lfoEngine`/`regenerateMelody` call a company-wide edit would otherwise miss would reproduce the exact stale-cache bug Roadmap Phase 9's post-launch fixes already found and fixed for the single-robot case.

Only the one field that was changed propagates into `lastEditedOptions` — never the company's entire resolved snapshot. A user editing a single member robot's value afterward (even from that robot's own screen) changes only that robot; the company's `lastEditedOptions` and every other member robot are untouched. There is no ongoing link between a robot and its company's settings after the broadcast — reassigning a robot to a different company, or leaving it in the same one, doesn't retroactively apply anything.

The same "only the one changed thing" rule applies one level deeper for compound fields (`volumeLfo`, `rhythmicMotifLength`, `noteVariance`, `adsr`, `layers`, per-layer `lfoSettings`) — dragging Ping Contour's Attack slider, for instance, must not overwrite every member's own Decay/Sustain/Release with the panel's shared baseline. Every compound drawer (`Lfo`, `PingContourDrawer`, `StepperWithToggle`, `SignatureArrayDrawer`) always emits a *whole* replacement value built by spreading its `value` prop — which is `CompanyOptionsSection`'s resolved baseline, not any individual member's own state — with just the touched sub-field set. `useCompanyOptions` diffs the old vs. new value (`diffCompoundField`/`diffLayerField`, `src/systems/companyOptions.ts`) to isolate that single sub-field, then merges just it onto each member's own current value (via `resolveCompanyOptions(undefined, member)`, or the member's own `lfoSettings` entry) before calling the matching `applyXxx` — so a member's own untouched sub-fields survive a broadcast intact, even when they differ from the panel's baseline.

### Pan and Auto (Roadmap Phase 49, docs/specs/ROBOT_PAN.md §1.5)

`CompanyOptionsSnapshot` carries two more optional fields, `pan` (stored −1…1) and `panAuto`, and `resolveCompanyOptions` falls back to the first member's own values (`0` and `true` for a robot with neither). Their handlers, `onPanChange` and `onPanAutoChange`, live in `useCompanyOptions`, not in `CompanyOptionsSection`.

- **Moving the slider** (Auto off) calls `applyPan` once per member with the display number and records the clamped fraction in `pan`, so every member lands at one value.
- **Switching Auto off** calls `applyPanAuto(member, localeId, false)` per member, and each member's sound holds at its own live position, not at one shared value. The snapshot records `{ panAuto: false, pan: <resolved pan> }`. The members therefore sit at different pans until the slider is next touched, which puts them all at one value. This is the broadcast-not-link rule, not a bug, and the company screen shows the snapshot rather than any member.
- **Switching Auto on** calls `applyPanAuto(..., true)` per member and records exactly `{ panAuto: true }`; the stored `pan` is ignored while Auto is on.
- **While Auto is on, the company slider is read-only at the stored `pan`.** It shows no animated average of the members' live positions: the company screen never subscribes to `robotPanReadoutStore`.

### Portamento and the bus filter (Robot Articulation 50.1, docs/specs/ROBOT_ARTICULATION.md §1.8)

`CompanyOptionsSnapshot` carries four more optional fields, `portamento` (glide seconds), `filterFreq`, `filterQ` and `filterType` (the bus filter). `resolveCompanyOptions` falls back to the first member's own values through the engine's resolvers, so what the panels show is what the engine plays: `0` for no glide, Q 1, lowpass, and a cutoff of `0` ("no filter") read as the open top of the band. The envelope's `attackCurve` / `releaseCurve` need no field of their own: they live inside the existing whole-object `adsr`, which already broadcasts one changed thing at a time.

- **Portamento:** `onPortamentoChange` calls `applyPortamento` once per member and records `{ portamento }`.
- **Filter:** `onBusFilterChange(patch)` calls `applyBusFilter` once per member with just the field the user touched and records exactly that patch, so dragging Resonance never rewrites any member's own Cutoff or Type.
- **The Cutoff's LFO link** (`bus.frequency`) goes through the same `onLayerLfoFieldChange` path as a layer link (`onBusLinkChange` delegates to it under the `bus.frequency` target; the diff is applied member by member with `applyLayerLfoLink`), and its greyed-out state reads `busFilterLinksHeldOff`.

Broadcast-not-link applies as everywhere else: a member edited afterwards on its own screen keeps its own value, and the snapshot is not rewritten.

### Reverb and Delay sends (Robot Sends 50.2, docs/specs/ROBOT_SENDS.md §1.6 and §1.9)

`CompanyOptionsSnapshot` carries two more optional fields, `reverbSend` and `delaySend` (stored 0…1, as on `Robot`). `resolveCompanyOptions` falls back to the first member's own values (`0` for a robot with neither), and the company screen's Reverb and Delay sliders sit under Pan in the Levels block, in `AudioSettingSection`. Their handler lives in `useCompanyOptions`, beside `onVolumeChange`: since roadmap 55 one `onSendChange(field, pct)` serves all five sends.

- **Moving a slider** cancels any swell on that attribute for every member (`cancelSwellForRobotAttribute`), calls `applySend(member, localeId, field, pct)` once per member with the display percent, and records the clamped fraction (`clampSend`) in the snapshot, so every member lands at one value. With no members nothing is recorded.
- **The swell flag** on each slider (its entry in the `sendSwelling` record, from `useSendSwellingFlags`) comes from the **first member**, as `volumeSwelling` does. A company-wide swell moves every eligible member's send in lock-step in time, not in magnitude; a member whose send is `0` is not eligible and stays put.
- A value that is not a number (a `NaN` display figure) is dropped whole, and records nothing.

### Grit, Shimmer and Double sends (Robot Effect Sends 50.7, docs/specs/ROBOT_EFFECT_SENDS.md §1.6)

`CompanyOptionsSnapshot` carries three more optional fields, `gritSend`, `shimmerSend` and `doubleSend` (stored 0…1), on exactly the Reverb and Delay rules above: `resolveCompanyOptions` falls back to the first member's own values (`0` for a robot with none), and the company screen's Grit, Shimmer and Double sliders sit after Delay in the Levels block. The same `onSendChange` cancels each member's swell on that send, calls `applySend` once per member, and records the clamped fraction in the snapshot; their three entries in `sendSwelling` come from the first member. Broadcast-not-link, as every field: a bulk edit can give the whole company Grit, so the one-of-three rule a fresh robot seeds under does not hold for hand edits.

Broadcast-not-link applies as everywhere else, so members disagree until a slider is touched, after which every member sits at one value; the snapshot is not rewritten by a later single-robot edit. A company is not saved as a snapshot (only its name and membership are, see [SESSION_STORAGE.md](SESSION_STORAGE.md)), so a company move is saved as the per-robot sends it left behind.

### Dynamics, Rests and Accents (Robot Performance 50.5, docs/specs/ROBOT_PERFORMANCE.md §1.8)

`CompanyOptionsSnapshot` carries three more optional fields, `dynamics`, `rests` and `accents` (0…100 each, as on `Robot`). `resolveCompanyOptions` falls back to the first member's own value, then to the default (`50` / `0` / `0`, today's sound). Humanize is global, so it has no company field. The company screen shows Dynamics on the Levels panel and Rests and Accents on the Composition panel's Articulation row, through the same `AudioSettingSection` and `PingControlsCompositionSection` the robot screen renders. Their handlers, `onDynamicsChange`, `onRestsChange` and `onAccentsChange`, live in `useCompanyOptions` on `onDensityChange`'s plain-number shape.

- **Moving a slider** calls `applyDynamics` / `applyRests` / `applyAccents` once per member with the value and records exactly that one key in the snapshot. None is a swell attribute, so nothing is cancelled.
- **Rests and Accents regenerate each member's melody** (the actions do that, as `applyPitchRepeat` does): every member keeps its own seeded notes and only the marks move together. Dynamics writes the field alone; the engine reads it on every note.
- A member with none of the fields reads as `50` / `0` / `0`, and a recorded `0` is a real value, not a missing one.

Broadcast-not-link applies as everywhere else: a member edited afterwards on its own screen keeps its own value, and the snapshot is not rewritten.

## Company Membership

Reassignment happens through a `RadioButton` over the company list plus **Freelance** (`FREELANCE_VALUE`, a non-empty sentinel kept for defensiveness/symmetry — see `docs/specs/COMPANY_ASSIGNMENT_RADIO.md` §1.2; built by `buildCompanyAssignmentSchema`, `src/data/companyConfig.ts`), shown in two places, both ending in the same `localeStore.assignRobotToCompany(localeId, robotId, companyId | null)`:

- **The Move chip on a roster tile.** A frame-style `CabinetBox` (flat at rest, popped while engaged) beside the tile's face — in the `<li>`, never inside the face's button, so the two presses never nest; it stops its own press so the tile does not open. On the desk it shows on hover or focus and opens a modal Popover anchored on the tile; on the phone it carries its short label and opens a Dialog sheet with the same radio, and a 500 ms tap-hold on the tile (`MOVE_LONG_PRESS_MS`) opens the same sheet. Both portal into `ScreenViewport` and are titled "Move <name> to" (`company.assign`'s template). `uiStore.movePanelRobotId` is the open panel's robot (at most one). A pick calls the board's `useRosterGlide` handler: it records every tile's rect (GSAP `Flip.getState`), writes the store through `assignRobotToCompany`, and once React has re-rendered the rows `Flip.from`s the recorded state, so the tile glides to its new row (timeline `roster-move-<id>` in `timelineMap`; skipped under reduced motion; never touches audio — [ANIMATION_SYSTEM.md](ANIMATION_SYSTEM.md)). Focus follows the moved tile's face.
- **The company radio in the robot screen's avatar column**, with its help "?".

`assignRobotToCompany` is one atomic store transition: it updates the robot's own `companyId` and both the old and new company's `robotIds` together, including the case where old and new are the same company (a genuine bug found in code review before merge — re-selecting the currently-assigned company must remove-then-re-add the robot from `robotIds`, not just remove it; see `localeStore.test.ts`'s regression coverage). Deleting a company (`removeCompany`) clears `companyId` on every member robot first — every robot in a deleted company becomes Freelance, mirroring `removeLocale`'s existing per-robot cleanup-before-removal pattern.

The Move chip and the roster tile's face are separate interactive elements for a reason: a `RadioButton` option is itself interactive, and a press that bubbled into the tile's own activation handler would open the robot as well as moving it. (Through Phase 10, when reassignment was a `Select`, the guard had to be a `stopPropagation` handler rather than a DOM `closest()` check, since React re-propagates a Radix `Select` dropdown's portaled events along the *component* tree. Nothing portals out of the chip's press any more, but the chip still stops the press at the trigger.)

## Forbidden Patterns

- Don't apply a company-wide edit by writing directly to `Locale.robots` in bulk — go through the matching `robotOptionsActions.ts` function per member, so every accompanying `AudioEngine`/`lfoEngine`/`regenerateMelody` call happens too.
- Don't make company membership a live-linked override — a company edit broadcasts once; it never re-applies later, and a subsequent single-robot edit is never overwritten by anything company-related.
- Don't generate company count, size, membership, or names with `crypto.randomUUID()` or bare `Math.random()` — seed everything through the even draw (`getUniformSeededVal`) on the locale noise map, per [PROCEDURAL_GENERATION.md](PROCEDURAL_GENERATION.md). (`CompanyCrudControls`' Create-suggestion and its `crypto.randomUUID()` company ID are the one deliberate exception — a live, user-triggered action has no seed to derive from in the first place, unlike spawn-time generation.)
- Don't reuse `selectedRobotId` for company selection, and don't skip `isCompanyMember` in favor of forcing `isSelected` for every member robot — the two selection states are independent and must stay that way.
- Don't carry companies across a coordinates-changing reseed (a New Transmission commit or Scan on the off screen) (e.g. diffing old vs. new membership, trying to preserve a renamed company) — that kind of reseed regenerates Companies fresh, deliberately, same as the rest of the roster. An Attenuation-Style-only reseed already preserves everything, companies included, with no company-specific code needed for that case either.
- Don't re-run the aspect deal on a Move, a company edit or a rename, and don't make `loadedUnder` a live setting — it records where a fresh robot started; the deal is spawn-time, pure, and mirrored in the saved-session baseline, so a second application would read as overrides.
- Don't let spawn, the roster baseline and the deal disagree about a family: a member inherits its founder's fields in `spawnRobot` and in `generateRobotRosterBaseline` alike, and is dealt at its founder's index live and in the baseline alike, or an untweaked world stops diffing to `{}` and a company splits into several voices.
- Don't put a family into the melody recipe: a member plays its founder's rhythm because it has its founder's Density and Motif Length, and the rhythm stream is keyed on the world and the rhythm shape only ([MELODY_SYSTEM.md](MELODY_SYSTEM.md)), never on a robot's lineage or seed.
- Don't apply an aspect's bands anywhere but through its family's derive function (`deriveMelodyFields`, `deriveEnvelopeFields`, `deriveSendFields`, `deriveVoiceFields`, `deriveMotionFields`, `derivePerformanceFields`, chosen by `deriveFamilyFields`) under `resolveBands(theme, { [family]: aspect })` — the live pass and the baseline pass must stay one derivation, or an untweaked dealt world stops diffing to `{}`.
- Don't filter derived arrays (like a company's member robots) *inside* a Zustand selector callback (`useLocaleStore(s => robots.filter(...))`) — it returns a new array reference every call, which `useSyncExternalStore` reads as "always changed," causing an infinite re-render loop. Subscribe to the raw array and filter outside the selector instead (see `useCompanyOptions.ts`).
