Trace slabs: sessions as objects, and a tactile sleeve transport

Lore (2026-10-08): a saved session is a trace, a captured transmission; the file of them on the desk is the atlas; loading one is a retransmission (the code already calls it retransmitWorld). The object is a slab of the world's gem material with the trace cut into it; the content is light. A slab is dark in the file and lit when seated, and the screen's two light-beam rails carry the beam. Started as a paper punch card (still selectable as a face for comparison); the paper went when the lore did not support it. Companion to layout-rework / option B: with the transport and volume on the sleeve, the screen's slim header goes away entirely. Static, judged by eye. Desktop frames are 1600 × 900 at 68 %; the phone is 375 × 812.

Guardrail: this puts interactive controls in the sleeve AND interactive objects on the desk outside the tablet. That is a deliberate amendment of the UI Shell rule (screen = software, sleeve = device hardware, desk = objects), to be recorded in the intent doc, not relaxed quietly.

C1 — The card, full size

Face:

Cut corner for orientation. Header strip: who punched it, name, locale code and coordinates, date, and a short checksum. The face carries the payload; the face chosen here is used on every other frame too. Punch holes is 1960s paper, twelve rows by forty columns. Hex cells is a Maxicode-style grid with a bullseye finder, drawn on a slab of the world gem material. Etched trace runs the data as PCB-style conductive traces with pads, the lore word made literal. Colour facets is an HCCB-style triangle grid in the four trait colours, two bits per cell. All four hash the same fake payload; the app would use the real bytes.

C2 — The sleeve corner, desktop: power, counter, keys

Latched:

The counter window reads measure of day and local time. Keys are the cabinet language: raised when up, flat when down. PLAY and PAUSE latch each other out; REW, FF, CUT and EJECT are momentary. No header on the screen any more: mute and volume would be a thumbwheel on the sleeve edge.

C3 — The desk: cards are objects, so they live outside the tablet

The frame is the app's existing .real-world surface, not the screen. The tablet shrinks to leave a shelf. The slot is on the tablet's right edge, side-loaded, and the seated slab is lit: its spine glows as the stub (name, locale code, date) and the screen's two light-beam rails carry the beam. The atlas on the shelf stands slabs on their spines like a catalogue drawer, blanks included, so it scales past six. Lift one to see its face; drag it to the slot to seat it, or to the blanker to quench it (confirm dialog as today), after which it returns to the file as a blank. CUT ejects a newly cut slab from the slot onto the shelf. The off screen needs no picker any more, only the label.

C5 — Option 2: ONE card, the Atlas. A mini computer: pick on its display, it renders the hex, it seats

Card state:

One object instead of a drawer. The left half is the data face, the right half is a small monochrome display with the whole atlas on it: user traces, then factory presets (read-only). Pick one and the data half lights with its hex; slide it into the side slot and only the data half goes in, so the display stays out and readable: the active trace, its cue, and the soft keys. CUT on the tablet writes the current state to the card: the display asks for a name (suggested as today), then renders. BLANK and BEAM (copy link) are soft keys on the card. A received link shows up as a new row. All CRUD is on the card; the tablet never lists sessions again.

C6 — Option 2 on the phone: the card seats into the tablet's bottom edge, display half below

The data half goes up into the tablet; the display half below is the bottom sheet, justified physically. Scroll down to it.

C4 — Option 1 on the phone: the page is the desk

The tablet is the top of the page, the slot is on its bottom edge and the seated card's spine shows below it, and the card file and shredder sit further down. Scrolling down is looking at the desk. Tap a card to seat it; it slides up into the slot.
